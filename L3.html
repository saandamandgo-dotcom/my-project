1. Properties of Cellular Life

Universal Properties (All Cells)

  • 1. Metabolism: Open system (Nutrients in -> Wastes out).
  • 2. Growth: Increase in cell number (Division).
  • 3. Evolution: Genetic changes selected for fitness.

Special Properties (Some Cells)

  • 1. Motility: Self-propulsion.
  • 2. Differentiation: Forming spores (Survival).
  • 3. Communication: Chemical signals (Quorum sensing).

Evolution & Phylogeny Details

Mutations (Raw Material):

Occasional errors in DNA replication. Since bacteria are Haploid (single chromosome), mutations are expressed immediately, allowing rapid evolution (e.g., Antibiotic Resistance).

Phylogeny (Tree of Life):

Relationships determined by sequencing rRNA genes. Divides life into 3 Domains: Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya.

4. Cell Wall & Peptidoglycan

Peptidoglycan Structure (Murein)

Composition:

Polysaccharide chains composed of repeating units:

  • NAG: N-acetylglucosamine
  • NAM: N-acetylmuramic acid
  • Glycan Tetrapeptide: 4 amino acids attached to NAM.
Bonds & Cross-links:
  • Beta-1,4 Glycosidic bond: Connects NAM-NAG. This bond is Lysozyme-sensitive.
  • Peptide Cross-links: Connect chains together (Rigidity).

إنزيم الليزوزيم يكسر الرابطة بيتا-1،4 بين وحدات السكر.

Gram-Positive (+)

  • Thick Peptidoglycan (90%).
  • Contains Teichoic Acids (Negative charge) & Lipoteichoic acids.
  • No Outer Membrane.
  • No Periplasmic space (usually).
  • Color: Purple.

Gram-Negative (-)

  • Thin Peptidoglycan (10%).
  • Has Outer Membrane (LPS, Porins).
  • Has Periplasm.
  • Color: Pink/Red.
LPS (Lipopolysaccharide):
1. Lipid A (Endotoxin)
2. Core Polysaccharide
3. O-Antigen (Protection/Specificity)
The Periplasm (Gram -ve):

Space between Cytoplasmic & Outer Membrane (15nm). Contains:

  • Hydrolytic Enzymes: Degrade food.
  • Binding Proteins: Transport substrates.
  • Chemoreceptors: Chemotaxis response.

5. Cytoplasmic Membrane

Structure

  • Phospholipid Bilayer: Fluid Mosaic Model.
  • Hopanoids: Strengthen membrane (like cholesterol in eukaryotes).
  • Proteins: Integral, Transmembrane, Peripheral.

Functions

  1. Permeability Barrier: Prevents leakage.
  2. Protein Anchor: Transport & Bioenergetics.
  3. Energy Conservation: Generation of Proton Motive Force (PMF).

6. Transport & Secretion

1. Simple Transport

Driven by Proton Motive Force (H+).
(Symport, Antiport, Uniport).

2. Group Translocation

Chemical modification. Driven by PEP.
Ex: Phosphotransferase (Glucose).

3. ABC Transporter

Periplasmic binding proteins + ATP.

Protein Export (Translocases)

Bacteria secrete Exoenzymes (e.g., Amylase to digest starch) and Toxins.

Sec System: General export/insertion into membrane.
Type III Secretion: "Syringe-like" system. Injects toxins directly into host cell.
