Exploring S-layers, Capsules, Flagella motility, Storage granules, and the survival mechanism of Endospores.

1. Cell Surface Layers

Structures external to the cell wall that provide protection and attachment.

S-Layer (Paracrystalline)

Interlocking protein or glycoprotein molecules. Common in Archaea.

  • Protection from osmosis.
  • Selective sieve (filter).
  • Retains proteins near cell surface.

طبقة بروتينية بلورية توجد غالباً في الأركيا للحماية والترشيح.

Capsule & Slime Layer

Sticky polysaccharide or protein layers.

Capsule Tight matrix, firmly attached, excludes particles (India ink).
Slime Layer Loose, easily deformed, difficult to see.
Functions: 1. Protection from Phagocytosis (Immune cells).
2. Protection from Desiccation (Drying).
3. Attachment to surfaces (Biofilms).

2. Fimbriae & Pili

Fimbriae (Short)

Filamentous protein structures. Short and numerous.

Function: Adhesion (sticking) to surfaces or tissues.

Pili (Long)

Longer and fewer (1-10 per cell).

  • Genetic Exchange: "Sex Pili" for Conjugation.
  • Motility: Type IV pili (Twitching motility).
  • Adhesion: Specific attachment.

3. Flagella & Motility

Flagellar Structure (Bacterial Propeller)

1. Filament: Rigid, hollow, helical. Made of Flagellin protein.
2. Hook: Connects filament to the motor.
3. Motor (Basal Body): Embedded in membrane. Powered by Proton Motive Force (PMF).
Note: Flagella rotate like a propeller (Rotor/Stator), they do not whip like eukaryotic flagella.

Flagellar Arrangements

Monotrichous Single polar flagellum
Lophotrichous Tuft at one end
Amphitrichous Both ends
Peritrichous All around cell

Taxis: Directed Movement

Chemotaxis:

Response to chemicals. "Run" (CCW rotation) and "Tumble" (CW rotation). Move towards attractants (food) or away from repellents.

Phototaxis:

Response to light (e.g., in Phototrophic bacteria).

4. Cell Inclusions

Energy reserves and building blocks stored inside the cell (Granules).

Carbon Storage
  • PHB: Poly-β-hydroxybutyrate (Lipid).
  • Glycogen: Glucose polymer.
Inorganic Storage
  • Polyphosphate: Source of Phosphate/Energy.
  • Sulfur Granules: In sulfur bacteria.
Magnetosomes

Magnetic iron oxides ($Fe_3O_4$). Allow orientation in magnetic fields (Magnetotaxis).

Gas Vesicles

Spindle-shaped, gas-filled. Confer Buoyancy (float) in planktonic bacteria.

5. Endospores

Survival Structures

Highly differentiated cells resistant to heat, harsh chemicals, and radiation. Formed by Bacillus and Clostridium during nutrient limitation.

Why are they resistant?
  • Dehydrated Core: 10-25% water (inactivates enzymes).
  • DPA + Ca²⁺: Dipicolinic Acid complexed with Calcium (stabilizes DNA).
  • SASPs: Small Acid-Soluble Proteins (bind DNA, protect from UV).
  • Thick Coat & Cortex.

Spore Structure (Out to In)

  • Exosporium: Thin protein outer covering.
  • Spore Coat: Layers of spore-specific proteins (Chemical resistance).
  • Cortex: Loosely cross-linked Peptidoglycan.
  • Core: Contains Core wall, Membrane, DNA, Ribosomes, SASPs, DPA.

اللب (Core) هو أهم جزء ويحتوي على المادة الوراثية ووسائل الحماية.

Life Cycle

Sporulation (Formation)

Triggered by stress (lack of food). Takes ~8 hours. Complex genetic process (Stages I - VII). Asymmetric cell division.

Germination (Return to life)

Triggered by nutrients (amino acids/sugars). Rapid process (minutes).

  1. Activation (Heat shock).
  2. Germination (Loss of resistance/refractility).
  3. Outgrowth (Swelling & Synthesis).
