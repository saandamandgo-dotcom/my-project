Studying Eukaryotic, Spore-bearing, Heterotrophic organisms. From Yeasts to Mushrooms.

1. What are Fungi?

Definition

Mycology (Mycota) is the study of fungi. They are a diverse group of Eukaryotes (True nucleus) that include yeasts, molds, and mushrooms.

Key Characteristics

  • Eukaryotic: Have organelles & nucleus.
  • Achlorophyllous: No Chlorophyll (Not plants).
  • Cell Wall: Composed of Chitin (not cellulose).
  • Storage: Store food as Glycogen.
  • Habitat: Cosmopolitan (Soil, Water, Plants, Animals).
  • Respiration: Aerobic (Require O2 & Water). No obligate anaerobes.

2. Forms & Structure (Thallus)

1

Unicellular (Yeasts)

Single cells (e.g., Saccharomyces). Reproduce by budding.

2

Multicellular (Molds)

Filamentous fungi. Consist of thread-like Hyphae forming a mass called Mycelium.

3

Dimorphic

Exist in two forms depending on conditions (Temp/Place).
Yeast in tissue (37°C) / Mold in soil (25°C).

Structure of Molds (Hyphae)

Based on Septation (Cross-walls):
  • Septate: Divided by cross-walls (septum) with pores. (Higher fungi).
  • Non-septate (Coenocytic): Continuous tube, multi-nucleated. (Lower fungi like Zygomycetes).
Based on Function:
  • Vegetative Mycelium: Penetrates substrate to absorb food.
  • Aerial (Reproductive) Mycelium: Projects above surface to produce spores.

3. Nutrition

Absorptive Heterotrophs

Fungi cannot make their own food. They secrete enzymes (Exoenzymes) to digest food outside their body, then absorb the nutrients.

1. Saprophytic Feed on dead organic matter (Decomposers).
2. Parasitic Feed on living hosts (cause disease).
3. Symbiotic Mutual benefit (e.g., Lichens = Fungi + Algae).

4. Reproduction

Fungi reproduce by Spores (Asexual & Sexual). The spore is the unit of dispersal.

A. Asexual Reproduction (Anamorph)

Common. Occurs by mitosis.

  • Budding: In Yeasts (unequal division).
  • Fragmentation: Hyphae break into pieces.
  • Spore Formation:
    • Sporangiospores: Inside a sac (Sporangium). Endogenous.
    • Conidia: Naked on tip of conidiophore. Exogenous.
    • Chlamydospores: Thick-walled resting spores.

B. Sexual Reproduction (Teleomorph)

Occurs under stress. Fusion of two nuclei.

Steps:
  1. Plasmogamy: Fusion of cytoplasm (n + n).
  2. Karyogamy: Fusion of nuclei (2n Zygote).
  3. Meiosis: Reduction division (Restore n).
  • Zygospores (Resistant).
  • Ascospores (In Ascus sac).
  • Basidiospores (On Basidium club).
Fungi Imperfecti (Deuteromycetes): Fungi with NO known sexual stage.

5. Classification

Based on the type of Sexual Spore.

Division Sexual Spore Hyphae Example
1. Zygomycotina Zygospore Non-septate (Coenocytic) Rhizopus (Bread mold)
2. Ascomycotina Ascospore (in Ascus) Septate Penicillium, Yeasts, Truffles
3. Basidiomycotina Basidiospore (on Basidium) Septate Mushrooms, Rusts
4. Deuteromycotina None (Absent) Septate Alternaria (Fungi Imperfecti)

6. Importance

Beneficial Effects

  • Decomposition: Nature's recyclers (Nutrient recycling).
  • Industry:
    • Antibiotics (Penicillin, Cephalosporin).
    • Food (Mushrooms, Cheese, Bread, Alcohol).
    • Enzymes & Acids (Citric acid).
  • Research: Model organisms for genetics.

Harmful Effects

  • Destruction: Spoilage of food, paper, cloth, lumber.
  • Diseases (Mycoses):
    • Superficial: Top skin/hair.
    • Cutaneous: Skin, hair, nails (Ringworm).
    • Subcutaneous: Under skin.
    • Systemic: Deep organs (Serious).
    • Opportunistic: In weak immunity (Candidiasis).
  • Toxins: Poisonous mushrooms, Mycotoxins (Aflatoxin).
