Antimicrobial Drugs & Chemotherapy

المضادات الحيوية والعلاج الكيميائي

The final frontier: Understanding how we fight microbes (Antibiotics), how they work (Mechanisms), and how microbes fight back (Resistance).

1. Definitions & Principles

Key Terms

  • Chemotherapeutic Drug: Any chemical used in treatment/prophylaxis of disease.
  • Antibiotics: Natural substances produced by microorganisms (like fungi/bacteria) to inhibit others.
  • Synthetic Drugs: Made in lab (e.g., Sulfa drugs).
  • Semisynthetic: Natural antibiotics chemically modified in lab.

Core Principle: Selective Toxicity

The drug must kill or inhibit the microbial target without damaging the host (human cells).

2. Classification of Antimicrobials

A. Spectrum of Activity

Narrow Spectrum

Effective against specific group (e.g., Only Gram-positive like Penicillin G).

Broad Spectrum

Effective against wide range (Gram +ve & -ve) like Tetracycline.

Disadvantage: Kills normal flora -> Superinfection (e.g., Candida).

B. Mechanism of Effect

Bactericidal

Kills the bacteria directly (Irreversible).

قاتل للبكتيريا.

Bacteriostatic

Inhibits growth (Reversible). Host immune system finishes the job.

مثبط للنمو (يوقف التكاثر).

3. Modes of Action

Antimicrobials target essential structures or functions in the bacterial cell.

1. Cell Wall Synthesis

Prevents Peptidoglycan formation (Weak wall -> Lysis).

Examples:
• Penicillins (Beta-lactams)
• Cephalosporins
• Bacitracin
• Vancomycin
2. Protein Synthesis

Targets 70S Ribosomes (30S or 50S subunit).

Examples:
• Chloramphenicol (50S)
• Erythromycin (50S)
• Tetracyclines (30S)
• Streptomycin (30S)
3. Nucleic Acid

Inhibits DNA replication or mRNA transcription.

Examples:
• Quinolones/Ciprofloxacin (Inhibit DNA Gyrase)
• Rifampin (Inhibits mRNA)
4. Plasma Membrane

Causes injury/leakage. Useful against Gram-negative.

Examples:
• Polymyxin B
(Note: Can be toxic to humans)
5. Essential Metabolites

Competitive inhibition of enzymes (e.g., Folic acid synthesis).

Examples:
• Sulfanilamide
• Trimethoprim

4. Antibiotic Resistance

How Bacteria Resist?

  • 1.
    Enzymatic Destruction: Producing enzymes like Beta-lactamase (Penicillinase) that destroy the drug.
  • 2.
    Prevention of Penetration: Modifying surface receptors/porins so drug can't enter (Reduced Permeability).
  • 3.
    Alteration of Target Site: Changing the ribosome or enzyme shape so drug can't bind.
  • 4.
    Efflux Pumps: Actively pumping the drug OUT of the cell.
  • 5.
    Metabolic Bypass: Using alternative pathways to avoid the blocked enzyme.

Strategies to Combat Resistance

  • Antimicrobial Stewardship Using drugs only when necessary and prescribed appropriately.
  • Combination Therapy Using multiple drugs (e.g., Antibiotic + Beta-lactamase inhibitor) to overcome resistance.
  • Vaccines Preventing infections reduces the need for antibiotics.
  • Research (New Drugs) Developing novel compounds.
