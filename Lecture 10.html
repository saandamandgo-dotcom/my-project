Evolution, Modern Synthesis, Speciation, and Hardy-Weinberg

1. Basic Definitions

Species (النوع)

Group of similar organisms that interbreed to produce fertile viable offspring.

Population (العشيرة)

Group of organisms of the same species living in the same area at the same time.

Gene Pool

The collective genetic information contained within a population.

2. Darwin & Mendel

Darwin's Natural Selection

Organisms better adapted tend to survive and reproduce.

  • Key Concept: Differential survival based on phenotype.
  • Divergence: Changes lead to divergent organisms (sharing a common ancestor).
  • 3 Principles: Variation is Random, Heritable, and changes in frequency.

Mendelian Inheritance (1865)

Mendel discovered Particulate Inheritance:

"Inheritance of discrete characters via genes that are independently expressed without the blending of characters from one generation to the next."

وراثة الجسيمات: توريث صفات محددة عبر الجينات التي يتم التعبير عنها بشكل مستقل دون "امتزاج" الصفات.

3. Modern Evolutionary Synthesis

Integration of Darwin & Mendel (1930s-40s)

Mendel's Insights
  • Heredity
  • Mechanics of inheritance (Genes)
  • Particulate Inheritance
+
Darwin's Insights
  • Evolution
  • Natural Selection
  • Variation

Result: Evolution defined as "Changes in allele frequencies within populations".

Five Basic Steps of Evolution (T. Dobzhansky):

1
Gene Variability (التنوع الجيني)

Variations present due to genetic changes. Causes:

Mutations: Sudden heritable changes (permanent change in genetic material).
Recombination: Exchange of genetic material during crossing over (Meiosis).
Hybridization: Two species are crossed -> offspring is Hybrid.
Genetic Drift: Elimination of genes/alleles by chance events (like disease or natural enemies).
2
Heredity

Transfer of characters from parents to offspring.

3
Natural Selection

Differential reproduction. Useful genes become predominant.

4
Reproductive Isolation

Prevention of interbreeding (Geographical, Temporal, Behavioral).

5
Speciation

Formation of new and distinct species.

4. Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

The Theory (1908)

Allele frequencies remain constant in the absence of evolutionary influences.

Microevolution (Disturbing Factors):
  • • Mutation
  • • Natural Selection
  • • Gene Flow (Migration)
  • • Genetic Drift (Change in population size)

* Microevolution can occur for a single locus while others remain at equilibrium.
