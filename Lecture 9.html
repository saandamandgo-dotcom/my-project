1. How Cancer Propagates & DNA Damage

The Mechanism

  • Root Cause: Mutation in one or more genes.
  • Effect: Incorrect proteins are translated.
  • Result: Uncontrollable multiplication -> Cancerous cells.
  • Timeline: A single mutation is rarely enough. Multiple mutations must accumulate over a lifetime (Explains why older people are at higher risk).

Sources of DNA Damage

Exogenous (External)
  • Radiation (UV, X-rays)
  • Viral Infections
  • Chemicals (Chemo)
Endogenous (Internal)
  • Replication stress
  • Oxygen radicals
  • Metabolic byproducts

2. Cell Cycle Control System

Crucial Concept: Two Irreversible Points

There are two points in the cell cycle from which there is no return:

  1. Replication of genetic material (تضاعف المادة الوراثية).
  2. Separation of sister chromatids (انفصال الكروماتيدات الشقيقة).
G1 Checkpoint

Checks cell size & DNA integrity. If failed -> G0 Resting Stage.

G2 Checkpoint

Checks if DNA replication is complete and correct.

M Checkpoint

Checks chromosome attachment to spindle fibers.

4. Types of Genes Linked to Cancer

A. Proto-oncogenes

Role: Stimulate cell growth (Gas pedal).

Mutation: Become Oncogenes (Gas pedal stuck). Usually acquired.

Example: RAS gene

B. Tumor-Suppressor Genes

Role: Inhibit cell division (Brakes). If mutated, brakes fail.

1. Gatekeepers

Directly suppress growth.

  • Checkpoint control
  • Apoptosis genes
2. Caretakers

Maintain genetic stability.

  • DNA repair proteins
  • Damage surveillance
Example: p53, BRCA1, BRCA2

5. The Role of p53

Sequence of Action (Slide 16):
  1. Stress Signal: DNA damage or Hypoxia detected.
  2. Activation: p53 is activated.
  3. Arrest: Forces cell into G1 arrest or G0 resting stage.
  4. Repair Attempt: Stimulates repair enzymes.
  5. Decision:
    • If fixed -> Restart Cell Cycle.
    • If NOT fixed -> Apoptosis (Death).

Mutated p53

Cell does NOT stop. Divides with damaged DNA. Mutations accumulate.
>50% of cancers involve p53 mutation.

8. Targeted Therapies

A. Drug Types

  • Small-molecule drugs: Enter cells easily. Target inside.
  • Monoclonal antibodies: Lab-made proteins. Attach to target ON cell surface.
    • Mark for immune destruction.
    • Stop growth.
    • Carry toxins.

B. Challenges

  • Resistance: Target changes structure OR cancer finds new pathway.
  • Hard to Develop: Target structure is complex.
  • Solution: Combination Therapy (Targeted + Chemo + Radiation).
