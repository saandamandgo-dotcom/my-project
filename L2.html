1. What is a Virus?

Meaning & Science

Word derived from Latin "Venom" (poisonous fluid). The science is Virology (branch of Microbiology).

كلمة فيروس مشتقة من كلمة لاتينية تعني "السم". العلم يسمى "علم الفيروسات".

Key Characteristics

  • Obligate intracellular parasites: Only function inside a living cell.
  • Acellular: Not a cell. No metabolism. Do not grow. Do not respire.
  • Nucleic Acid: DNA OR RNA (Never both).
  • Size: 20-300 nm (Much smaller than bacteria). Detected by Electron Microscope.

Living vs Non-living? (بين الحياة والجماد)

Inside Host (Living) Multiplication, Genetic Material.
Outside Host (Non-living) Crystallizable, No metabolism.

2. Structure & Shapes

1. Capsid (Protein Coat)

Made of protein subunits called Capsomeres. Protects genome & determines host specificity.

2. Nucleic Acid (Core)

Central core. DNA or RNA. Responsible for the infective nature.

3. Envelope (Optional)

Outer lipid layer with Spikes. (Enveloped vs Naked/Non-enveloped viruses).

Nucleocapsid: Capsid + Nucleic acid.

Virion: A virus particle which is non-replicating outside a host cell.

Virus Morphologies (الأشكال)

Spherical Influenza
Rod (Helical) TMV (Tobacco Mosaic)
Polyhedral Adenovirus (Cuboid)
Complex Bacteriophage (Tadpole)
Filamentous Ebola-like

3. Host & Specificity

Host Specificity

  • Animal Viruses: Hepatitis, Influenza.
  • Plant Viruses.
  • Mycophages: Infect Fungi.
  • Bacteriophages: Infect Bacteria.
  • Cyanophages: Infect Algae.
Tissue Tropism: Viruses infect specific tissues (e.g., Flu -> Respiratory tract). Specificity depends on surface proteins binding to host receptors.

Example: SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19)

Structure Proteins:
  • S (Spike): Binds to ACE2 receptor.
  • N (Nucleocapsid): Protects RNA.
  • M (Membrane).
  • E (Envelope).
Characteristics:
  • Enveloped, RNA virus.
  • Size: 75-150 nm.
  • Inactivation: 70% Ethanol, Soap, UV, Heat (>Cooking temp).

Modes of Transmission

Aerosols Ingestion Injection Sexual Contact Bites

4. Replication & Cultivation

Viral Replication Steps (Animal Viruses)

1

Adsorption: Specific attachment to receptors.

2

Penetration (Viropexis): Virus enters the cell.

3
Uncoating: Release of genetic material. Note: Removal of capsid is done by lysosomal proteases enzymes.
4

Replication: Synthesis of viral parts (Nucleic acid then Protein coat).

5

Assembly: Putting parts together.

6

Release: Lysis (bursting) or Budding (for enveloped).

Cultivation Methods

1. Living Animals
Inoculation into mice, rabbits, etc.
2. Embryonated Eggs
Inoculation into specific sites:
  • Yolk sac
  • Amniotic cavity
  • Allantoic cavity
  • Chorioallantoic membrane
3. Tissue Culture
Cells grown in lab media.

5. Bacteriophages

Viruses that infect bacteria. Potential use: Phage Therapy (Alternative to antibiotics).

Structure (Detailed):
  • Capsid Head
  • Nucleic Acid
  • Collar
  • Whiskers
  • Sheath
  • Base Plate
  • Tail Fibers
  • Spikes
Life Cycles:
1. Lytic Cycle (Virulent): Virus attaches, enters, replicates, and bursts (lyses) the cell.
2. Lysogenic Cycle (Temperate): Virus integrates into host DNA as a Prophage. Replicates with bacteria. Can turn Lytic.

6. Viruses & Cancer

Oncogenic Viruses

Integrate genetic material into host DNA, disrupting Oncogenes or Tumor Suppressor Genes.

Cancer Signs:
  • 1. Hyperplasia: Uncontrolled proliferation.
  • 2. Anaplasia: Structural abnormality.
  • 3. Metastasis: Spread to other sites.

Treatment Strategies

Antiviral Drugs:

Target: Entry, Nucleic acid synthesis, or Assembly/Release.

Vaccines:
Live-Attenuated Inactivated (Killed) Subunit mRNA
