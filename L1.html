1. What is Microbiology?

Definition

Science studying living things < 1mm (germs/bugs). Includes Bacteria, Viruses, Fungi (Yeast/Mold), Algae, Protozoa, and Prions.

العلم الذي يدرس كائنات حية أقل من 1 مم (جراثيم). تشمل البكتيريا، الفيروسات، الفطريات، الطحالب، الأوليات، والبريونات.

Scale

  • Human Eye: > 1 mm
  • Light Microscope: 1 µm - 100 µm (Bacteria/Cells)
  • Electron Microscope: 1 nm - 100 nm (Viruses)

2. Scope & Fields

Applied Microbiology (علوم تطبيقية)

• Medical / Public Health • Pharmaceutical • Industrial / Biotech • Food & Dairy • Agricultural / Veterinary • Environmental

3. Importance of Microorganisms

1

The Progenitor (Evolution)

First bacteria appeared ~3.5 billion years ago. Ancestors of all life.

أول بكتيريا ظهرت قبل 3.5 مليار سنة، وهي أصل الحياة.

2

Environment Balance

  • Photosynthesis: Produce >50% of Earth's oxygen (Algae/Cyanobacteria).
  • Decomposition: Recycle nutrients (N2 fixation).
3

Industrial Uses

  • Bioleaching: Extracting metals from ores using microbes.
  • Bioremediation: Cleaning oil spills/sewage.
  • Synthesis: Drugs, enzymes, alcohol.
4

Food Industry

Production of Vinegar, Yogurt, Cheese, Bread, Pickles.

إنتاج الخل، الزبادي، الجبن، الخبز.

5

Biotechnology

  • Genetic Engineering: Recombinant DNA.
  • Products: Human Insulin, Vaccines.
  • Gene Therapy: Replacing defective genes.
6

The Microbiome

"Microbiota": Microbes living on/in us.

  • Aid digestion.
  • Synthesize Vitamins (B, K).
  • Prevent pathogen growth.
  • Train immune system.

Harmful Effects (الأضرار)

Infectious Diseases:

Malaria, TB, AIDS, Hepatitis. (Responsible for significant % of global deaths).

Other Harms:

Food Spoilage (Economic loss), Antibiotic resistance.

4. Nomenclature & Classification

Binomial Nomenclature (Carolus Linnaeus)

System: Genus species (Italicized or Underlined). Genus capitalized, species lowercase.

Staphylococcus aureus
Staphylo (clustered) + coccus (spheres) + aureus (golden)
Escherichia coli
Theodor Escherich + coli (colon)
Feature Prokaryotes (Bacteria) Eukaryotes (Fungi/Plants/Animals)
Nucleus Poorly defined, No membrane (Nucleoid) Well defined, Nuclear membrane present
Organelles Absent (No Mitochondria/Golgi) Present (Mitochondria, Golgi, etc.)
Cellularity Mostly Unicellular Mostly Multicellular
Bacteria Peptidoglycan wall. Prokaryotes.
Archaea Extreme environments (Methanogens, Halophiles). No Peptidoglycan.
Eukarya Protists, Fungi, Plants, Animals.
Viruses? Acellular (DNA/RNA + Protein coat). Not in 3 domains.

5. History of Microbiology

1665

Robert Hooke

Cell Theory: Observed cork, coin "Cells" (Little boxes).

1673

Antonie van Leeuwenhoek

First to see living microbes ("Animalcules") using simple microscope.

1668

Francesco Redi (Vs Spontaneous Gen.)

Opposed "Abiogenesis". Experiment: Meat in jars (Open vs Covered). Maggots came from flies, not meat.

عارض التوالد التلقائي بتجربة اللحم والذباب.

1861

Louis Pasteur (Father of Micro)

  • • Disproved Spontaneous Gen: Swan-neck flask experiment.
  • • Pasteurization: Heat to kill microbes (Wine/Milk).
  • • Fermentation: Caused by yeast.
  • • Vaccines: Rabies & Anthrax.
  • • Aseptic Tech: Cotton plugs.
1860s

Joseph Lister

Antiseptic Surgery: Used Phenol (Carbolic acid) to clean wounds. Reduced infection.

1876

Robert Koch (Germ Theory)

Linked specific microbe to specific disease (Anthrax).

Koch's Postulates:
1. Microbe present in all sick hosts.
2. Isolated in pure culture.
3. Causes disease in healthy host.
4. Re-isolated again.
1900s

Chemotherapy Era

Paul Ehrlich: "Magic Bullet" (Salvarsan) for Syphilis.

Alexander Fleming (1928): Discovered Penicillin (First Antibiotic).
