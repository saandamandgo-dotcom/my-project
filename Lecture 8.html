1. Nature & Classification

طبيعة وتصنيف الطفرات

Evolutionary Significance

Mutations are the primary source of Genetic Variation. Without mutations, evolution cannot occur.
Classic Example: The elongation of the giraffe's neck was driven by beneficial mutations selected over generations.

الطفرات هي المصدر الرئيسي للتنوع الجيني، وبدونها لا يحدث التطور. مثال: استطالة عنق الزرافة كانت نتيجة طفرات مفيدة تم اختيارها طبيعياً.

Somatic Mutations

Non-Inherited

طفرات الخلايا الجسدية

  • Occur in body tissues (Skin, Liver, Muscle).
  • Effect: Affects only the individual (e.g., Skin Cancer).
  • Scope: Only tissues derived from the mutated cell via Mitosis are affected (Mosaicism).
  • Inheritance: NEVER passed to offspring.

Germ Cell Mutations

Inherited

طفرات الخلايا التناسلية

  • Occur in Gametes (Sperm/Egg) or their precursors.
  • Effect: May not affect the parent, but defines the offspring.
  • Scope: Every single cell in the new organism will carry this mutation.
  • Inheritance: Passed to future generations.

2. Substitution Mutations

طفرات الاستبدال (تغيير كيميائي)

A. Transition (الأكثر شيوعاً)

Replacing a base with another of the same chemical family (Ring structure remains similar).

Purine ↔ Purine (A ↔ G)

Pyrimidine ↔ Pyrimidine (C ↔ T)

3. Frameshifts & Repeats

Frameshift Mutation

Insertion or Deletion of bases (not divisible by 3) shifts the "Reading Frame" of the ribosome.

Normal Sentence (Codons of 3):

THE FAT CAT SAT

Delete 'F' (Frameshift):

THE ATC ATS AT...

Total gibberish downstream!

تخيل الجملة الوراثية ككلمات ثلاثية. حذف حرف واحد يغير تقسيم كل الكلمات التي تليه، مما ينتج بروتيناً عشوائياً تماماً وغالباً ما ينتهي بوقوف مبكر.

Trinucleotide Repeat Expansion

Mechanism: Replication Slippage. DNA polymerase "slips" and copies the same section multiple times.

Huntington's Disease (HD)
  • Repeated Sequence: CAG (Codes for Glutamine).
  • Normal: 10-26 repeats.
  • Pathogenic: >40 repeats.
  • Effect: Creates a "Poly-Glutamine" tail in the Huntingtin protein. This makes the protein sticky/toxic, killing brain neurons.

4. Spontaneous Causes

أسباب تلقائية (كيميائية)
1. Tautomeric Shift

Keto Form (Normal) ↔ Enol Form (Rare)

Bases exist in equilibrium between two shapes. If replication happens while a base is in its rare Enol form, it pairs wrongly.
Ex: Rare 'G' pairs with 'T' (instead of C).

تغير لحظي في توزيع ذرات الهيدروجين يغير قدرة القاعدة على الارتباط، فتتزوج مع الخطأ.

2. Depurination

Loss of A or G

Spontaneous hydrolysis breaks the bond between the base and sugar.
Result: Apurinic Site (AP site). A hole in the DNA code.
Replication stalls or inserts a random base here.

انفصال قاعدة البيورين تلقائياً عن السكر، مما يترك مكاناً فارغاً يسبب الحيرة للإنزيم الباني.

3. Deamination

Cytosine → Uracil

Loss of an amino group (-NH2). Cytosine turns into Uracil (which belongs in RNA!).
Danger: Uracil pairs with Adenine.
Result: C-G pair becomes T-A pair in next gen.

تحول كيميائي للسيتوزين يجعله يوراسيل. إذا لم يُصلح، سيتم قراءته كأنه ثايمين وتتغير الشفرة.

INDUCED

Mutagens: UV Light (Causes Thymine Dimers), X-Rays (Break backbone), Chemicals (Intercalating agents).

5. DNA Repair Pathways

أنظمة الدفاع (الإصلاح)
Single Strand Damage
1. Mismatch Repair (MMR)

The Spell Checker

Works immediately after replication. It scans the newly synthesized strand for bumps caused by mismatches (e.g., G paired with T).

Cuts out the chunk of new strand and replaces it correctly.

2. Base Excision Repair (BER)

The Tweezer

Fixes small, non-bulky damage (e.g., Uracil in DNA).

  1. Glycosylase enzyme flips the damaged base out and cuts it off.
  2. Endonuclease cuts the sugar-phosphate backbone.
  3. Polymerase fills the 1-nucleotide gap.
3. Nucleotide Excision Repair (NER)

The Surgeon

Fixes Bulky Lesions that distort the helix (e.g., Thymine Dimers caused by UV).

Cuts a large "patch" (12-24 bases) around the damage, removes it, and rebuilds fresh.

Double-Strand Breaks (The most dangerous)

If the DNA snaps completely in half (e.g., from X-rays), the cell has two options:

A. Homologous Recombination (HR)
Error-Free

Requirement: Needs a sister chromatid (undamaged copy) nearby.
Timing: Only in S or G2 phase.
Process: Copies the sequence from the sister to repair the break perfectly.

B. Non-Homologous End Joining (NHEJ)
Error-Prone

Scenario: No sister chromatid available (G1 phase) or desperate emergency.
Process: Proteins just grab the two broken ends, trim them, and glue them back together.
Cost: Often loses a few bases (Mutation), but saves the chromosome from falling apart.
